PhD direction: passive acoustic inference for underwater monitoring

Deep-sea population surveys need sensing where light, communication, and access are constrained. Cameras and artificial lighting disturb animals and burn energy. My hypothesis: marine animals generate partially distinguishable sounds when they move, and a low-power hydrophone array can capture enough of that signature to infer species or coarse groups — enabling long-duration, low-disturbance, energy-efficient surveys.

This raises two research questions:

  1. How reliably can marine species be identified from movement acoustics alone?
  2. How can rare-event data collection be maximized by a system under energy constraints?

I've explored both questions in a series of simulation-based notebooks, published as Energy-Efficient Marine Inference.

Bird species classification

At Victoria University of Wellington I'm developing a pipeline for classifying bird species from their calls using Audio Spectrogram Transformers, with a focus on the domain shift problem across different recording environments — closely related to the acoustic classification challenges of my PhD.

Quitter — quadratic voting social media

A social media prototype where content is ranked by quadratic voting across repeated rounds. Users allocate positive or negative votes from a fixed credit budget, aligning outcomes with true preference rather than raw engagement. Built with Dash and released open-source. Live app · GitHub
